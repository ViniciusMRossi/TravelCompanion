# Travel Companion — Content Style Guide

Language default: **Brazilian Portuguese**

The product is a personal field companion, not a tourism brochure.

Write for a traveler who is already there, walking, looking around and often listening through headphones.

---

# 1. Voice

Aim for:

- curious;
- precise;
- conversational;
- intelligent without sounding academic;
- vivid without sounding promotional;
- concise enough to use while traveling.

Avoid:

- "cidade encantadora";
- "destino imperdível";
- "rica história e cultura";
- "joia escondida";
- empty superlatives;
- generic tourism copy;
- excessive adjectives;
- Wikipedia-like chronology dumps.

Prefer concrete observation.

Bad:

> Sarajevo é uma cidade fascinante, rica em cultura e história.

Better:

> Em poucas quadras, Sarajevo muda de linguagem arquitetônica. Minaretes otomanos, fachadas austro-húngaras e marcas do século XX aparecem quase na mesma linha de caminhada.

---

# 2. Write for physical presence

Whenever appropriate, connect information to something the traveler can:

- see;
- hear;
- touch safely;
- compare;
- walk toward;
- notice in scale or material.

Use:

- "Repare..."
- "Olhe para..."
- "Se você estiver diante..."
- "Ao atravessar..."
- "Observe..."

But only use left/right/turn-direction instructions when the walk approach/orientation is controlled and verified.

Do not give fragile spatial directions in general attraction content.

---

# 3. City content

City content should answer:

1. What makes this place distinct?
2. What historical layers explain what I am seeing?
3. What should I notice while walking?
4. Which stories connect today's itinerary?
5. What local context prevents me from seeing the city superficially?

Suggested targets:

### Intro
80–180 words.

### History chapters
2–6 chapters, usually 100–250 words each.

### City audio overview
Roughly 8–15 minutes when enough content exists.

Do not force length.

---

# 4. Attraction content

Structure around:

- concise summary;
- why it matters;
- practical information;
- history;
- interesting facts;
- what to observe;
- optional audio.

Suggested summary:

60–120 words.

Prefer 3–6 strong observations over 15 weak bullet points.

---

# 5. Stories pelo caminho

A Story is not a full attraction page.

It should feel like a rewarding discovery because the traveler happens to be there.

A good Story contains:

1. hook;
2. immediate physical context;
3. one memorable idea;
4. optional broader historical connection;
5. quick closing/next cue.

Typical audio target:

1–4 minutes.

### Notification/title hook

Short, specific and intriguing.

Bad:

> História da Ferhadija

Better:

> Aqui Sarajevo muda de império em poucos passos

Do not use clickbait.

---

# 6. Audio-guide writing

Audio is written for ears, not copied from the screen.

Prefer:

```text
context
→ spatial cue
→ observation
→ story
→ pause
→ next instruction
```

Allowed production-script annotations:

```text
[pause 2s]
[pause 4s]
[look ahead]
[look up]
[stop here]
[continue walking]
[pronunciation: ...]
```

Do not overload with annotations.

---

# 7. Walking-tour segments

The phone may be in the user's pocket.

Each segment should make sense without constant screen checks.

A segment may contain:

- where they are;
- what to notice;
- why it matters;
- what to do next.

Avoid long lists of names/dates while the traveler is moving.

Use dates only when they anchor the story.

---

# 8. Spatial directions

Spatial directions have safety implications.

Use them only when:

- route direction is known;
- approach to the location is predictable;
- the instruction has been checked against the planned walk.

Prefer stable cues:

> Continue até a ponte.

over fragile cues:

> Vire imediatamente à esquerda depois da banca azul.

Do not use audio as safety-critical turn-by-turn navigation.

Google Maps or another navigation app remains responsible for navigation.

---

# 9. Historical content

Give enough context to answer:

> Why does this matter here?

Avoid turning every place into a full history lecture.

Use chronology only when it clarifies what is visible.

When discussing sensitive history, war, ethnicity, religion or politics:

- use neutral factual language;
- avoid sensationalism;
- distinguish interpretation from fact;
- avoid flattening complex conflicts into hero/villain narratives.

---

# 10. Interesting facts

A fact should earn its place.

Good facts are:

- surprising;
- visible;
- connected to the location;
- useful for remembering the story.

Avoid trivia with no experiential payoff.

---

# 11. Practical content

Operational text should be direct.

Bad:

> É recomendável considerar chegar com uma certa antecedência para garantir maior tranquilidade.

Better:

> Esteja na estação às 19:00.

When a value is derived rather than official:

> Recomendação: chegar às 19:00, 30 min antes da saída.

---

# 12. Plan B

Plan B must reduce stress.

Use:

- calm language;
- numbered actions;
- clear decision points;
- concrete fallback;
- direct links/documents where relevant.

Bad:

> Procure outra alternativa de transporte.

Better:

> 1. Vá ao balcão da rodoviária e confirme a próxima saída para Mostar.
> 2. Se houver ônibus hoje, compre o próximo bilhete.
> 3. Avise a hospedagem do novo horário.
> 4. Se não houver opção segura, durma mais uma noite em Sarajevo.

Do not create fake fallback businesses or transport schedules.

---

# 13. Critical Items

Separate official fact from operational recommendation.

Example:

```text
Saída: 19:30
Recomendação: estar na estação às 19:00
```

Critical copy should be scannable in seconds.

Use verbs.

Examples:

- Saia do hotel até 18:25.
- Leve o passaporte.
- Deixe a passagem aberta offline.
- Ligue para o anfitrião se atrasar.

---

# 14. Weather fallback

Fallback weather describes expected seasonal conditions, not a precise forecast.

Good:

> Setembro costuma ter dias amenos e noites mais frescas. Leve uma camada leve e esteja preparado para chuva.

Bad:

> Vai chover às 15h.

Live weather belongs to the runtime weather service.

---

# 15. Outfit / carry suggestions

Use short concrete lists.

Inputs may include:

- temperature;
- rain;
- wind;
- altitude;
- walking duration;
- terrain;
- attraction rules.

Outputs:

- `wear`;
- `carry`;
- `special`.

Avoid fashion advice unless it has practical relevance.

---

# 16. Restaurants

Recommendation copy should explain why the option fits the trip.

Examples:

- 6 min da rota;
- bom para almoço rápido;
- cozinha bósnia tradicional;
- opção vegetariana;
- útil depois do passeio;
- costuma abrir mais tarde.

Avoid generic praise.

---

# 17. Pronunciation

For audio scripts, add pronunciation hints only where they materially improve TTS or traveler confidence.

Do not clutter visible app text with phonetics.

---

# 18. Repetition

Do not repeat the same historical paragraph across:

- city page;
- attraction;
- Story;
- walk audio.

Each format has a different job.

City:
context.

Attraction:
depth.

Story:
one memorable discovery.

Walk:
experience and movement.

---

# 19. Editorial self-check

Before accepting a piece of content, ask:

- Could this text be pasted into any other city? If yes, rewrite.
- Does it help because the traveler is physically here?
- Is there a concrete thing to notice?
- Are factual claims grounded?
- Is the content shorter than it needs to be?
- Is any detail duplicated elsewhere?
- Would this sound natural when spoken aloud?
